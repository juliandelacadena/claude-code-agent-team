# When in Rome — Brand Identity

Longer reference for voice, positioning, and content rules. `CLAUDE.md` has the short version
every agent reads first — this file is what Oppius and Curio lean on when they actually need
to write something.

## Positioning
When in Rome sits next to Uniqlo, Everlane, and J.Crew — 22–35, values fit and
fabric over logos, buys for versatility rather than a specific scene. The pitch: one capsule
that reads right whether you're at a desk, at dinner, or on a flight — "do as the Romans do,"
wherever that ends up being.

## Voice & tone
**Confident, plainspoken, a little wry.** In practice:
- **Confident** means stating things plainly — no exclamation points doing the emotional work.
- **Plainspoken** means describing the actual garment (fabric, cut, why it holds up) over
  abstract brand language.
- **A little wry** means the name's own joke is allowed to land in copy once in a while — never
  forced into every post.
- **Never precious.** No "artisan," no "curated," no paragraph about the founder's journey.

## Visual world
Clean, warm-neutral photography — travertine, aged brass, deep charcoal. Real locations over
studio backdrops: the product shown doing what it's for, not staged as an art object.

## Do's and don'ts
**Do:**
- Keep claims tied to something real. Missing a number or review → mark `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
- Write for someone who's never heard of the brand before — every post is a first impression.

**Don't:**
- Don't fabricate scarcity ("3 left!", fake countdowns). If a real scarcity angle exists, use
  it — it just has to be real.
- Don't lean on the name for every joke. One good "when in Rome" line beats five weak ones.
